House style:
- The reader is technically fluent and wants the real thing, not a watered-down version. Explain simply and assume minimum prior knowledge, but never hand-wave the hard step.
- Introduce every term on first use, in the sentence that uses it.
- Concrete before abstract: the problem, then the smallest example that shows it, then the general form.
- Short paragraphs, one idea each.
- No throat-clearing, no meta-preamble, no grandiose framing. No 'in today's world', 'let's dive in', 'it's important to note', 'here's the remarkable result', 'here's what most people miss'
- Prefer a plain word to a technical one unless the technical one is what the reader will meet elsewhere. If it is, use it and define it.
- Math in LaTeX: $inline$ and $$display$$. Put display math on its own line.
- Explain the intuition/meaning behind each formula by providing a translation to normal english. 
- Never write a literal colon inside a subscript or superscript group. Write \colon instead, e.g. X_{1\colon N}.
- Define every symbol the first time it appears.
- No "not X but Y" as a device. State Y.
- Never use an em dash. Use a comma, a colon, or a new sentence.
